Now that we’ve broken down the AFC, let's turn our attention to the NFC. This conference is heading toward total chaos, including wild tiebreakers, shocking upsets, and a 7-10 division winner hosting a playoff game. Yet at the top of the mountain, beating out the clear favorite might prove nearly impossible.

## NFC North

The NFC North has a lot of talent between the Bears, Lions, Packers, and even the Vikings. Unfortunately, only two of these teams can make it to the playoffs with how the rest of the NFC pans out. Detroit wins the division at 12-5, followed by Chicago and Green Bay, both at 10-7. This leads to the tiebreaker scenario where the 10-7 Packers miss the playoffs with Chicago winning the tiebreaker and sneaking into the final playoff spot.

This would lead to a third matchup between the #2 seeded Lions and the #7 seeded Bears. This is a tough game to make a pick in, with both teams winning at home against each other in the regular season, but one of them must come out victorious. The Lions have more than enough firepower and a great coach to go along with that, but Caleb Williams and the Bears, coached by Detroit’s former offensive coordinator Ben Johnson, have exactly what it takes to upset this Lions team and move on to the second round. The Divisional Round isn't as kind to Chicago, as their Cinderella run ends in a brutal matchup against my projected NFC Champion.

## NFC East

The NFC East has been well controlled by either the Dallas Cowboys or Philadelphia Eagles since Washington won with a 7-9 record in 2020. Even before that we must go back to 2015 to see a winner outside of Dallas and Philadelphia. This year that won’t be the case. 

Washington sits at the bottom after a disappointing 5-12 campaign. They might squeeze out seven wins at best, but they finish last either way. Next comes a two-way tie for second place that ends up meaningless, as both Dallas and Philadelphia miss the postseason at 8-9. Both heavyweights could steal another win or two, but roster issues and tough schedules keep them on the outside looking in.

That brings us to the division winner: the New York Giants claim the crown for the first time since 2011. Finishing 10-7, the Giants secure the #3 seed behind a serviceable defense and an offense loaded with Jaxon Dart, Cam Skattebo, Malik Nabers, Isaiah Likely, and Najee Harris. If they keep Dart protected, this unit can produce.

Unfortunately, the playoffs aren’t too friendly to the Giants. In the Wild Card round, they are most likely going to have to deal with the San Francisco 49ers, who are just better. With this Wild Card loss, the Giants season comes to an end but gives them hope for years to come.

## NFC South

Now let’s get to the best division in the league, the NFC South. Oh wait… scratch that. I meant the worst division in the league. This division has some hope for the future, with the Saints having a promising young QB in Tyler Shough and acquiring Travis Etienne this offseason.

Starting at the bottom, the Atlanta Falcons finish last in the division at 4-13. The Falcons possess the best player in the division in Bijan Robinson alongside solid skill positions. However, even with new head coach Kevin Stefanski bringing his experience from Cleveland, this offense won't produce without better quarterback play.

Tampa Bay finishes third with a record of 6-11. While they have the most well-rounded roster in the division, an ongoing contract dispute with Baker Mayfield and a projected regression from Bucky Irving compared to last year's production leave them in a bind. Combined with a tough schedule, they face an uphill battle all year that they will have trouble getting over. 

The division ends in a tie at the top, with New Orleans and Carolina both posting sub-.500 records at 7-10. New Orleans has a talented roster and the brightest future behind Tyler Shough, but this year isn't kind to them. Costly losses to Las Vegas and a season sweep by Atlanta leave them tied with Carolina, and losing the tiebreaker knocks the Saints out of the picture.

That brings us to the division winner. Carolina sneaks into the postseason at 7-10 and earns a home playoff game. Although the Panthers showed promise late last season when they nearly upset the Rams in the Wild Card round, losing Rico Dowdle hurts, and Bryce Young still hasn't consistently proven himself. In their Wild Card matchup against the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks, playing at home isn't enough. Carolina lacks the consistency and firepower to pull off the upset, ending their season in Round 1.

## NFC West

Now here is an elite division, the defending Super Bowl champion in Seattle, the NFC runner-up in the LA Rams, and the San Francisco 49ers who were dealing with multiple injuries to key players last year and still made the playoffs with a record of 12-5. Arizona does bring the conference down a little bit, but the top 3 teams are good enough to call this division the best division in the NFL currently. 

Unsurprisingly, Arizona sits at the bottom with a 1-16 record (3-14 ceiling). A brutal schedule, including six games against their division opponents, combined with mediocre quarterback play and an unimproved defense leaves little hope. It will be a long year for Cardinals fans, but landing a top draft pick provides a silver lining for their ongoing rebuild. 

The San Francisco 49ers finish 12-5 to secure the #6 seed in the NFC playoffs. Kyle Shanahan remains an elite play-caller, and even with concerns over Christian McCaffrey’s health following a massive 311-carry, 102-reception season, Shanahan always finds a way to keep this offense rolling. Meanwhile, San Francisco's defense remains top tier, anchored by Fred Warner and Nick Bosa, allowing them to control the tempo of almost any game.

In the Wild Card round, the 49ers face a favorable matchup against a surprising 10-7 New York Giants team. While New York enjoyed a great season, they aren't equipped to take down a juggernaut, leading to a San Francisco victory. Unfortunately for the 49ers, their journey ends in Round 2 when they run into the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks. San Francisco falls short once again, bringing an impressive season to a familiar close.

The defending Super Bowl champions roll over their dominance from last year and finish 13-4. In almost any other division, 13 wins claims the title with ease, but in a stacked NFC West, Seattle finishes just one game behind the LA Rams. Locking up key players like Devon Witherspoon, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and Derick Hall sets up another year with a dominant defense and an explosive offense. Losing Kenneth Walker was supposed to be a big hit for them, but Zach Charbonnet will return from injury at some point this year, and rookie Jadarian Price has been dominating training camp and looks like a respectable replacement for Walker.

As the #5 seed in the NFC playoffs, Seattle travels to Carolina in the Wild Card round, cruising past the 7-10 Panthers without much trouble. That sets up a Divisional Round rematch against the San Francisco 49ers for the third time this season. Facing a 12-win 49ers squad is a far tougher test than Carolina, but Seattle outlasts their division rival to advance.

The NFC Championship is a rematch of last year where the Seahawks once again face the LA Rams. This game is a thriller once again, with the winner positioned as the clear favorite heading into the Super Bowl.

Sean McVay’s team takes the NFC West title at 14-3 to lock up the #1 seed and a Wild Card bye. Fresh off his MVP season, Matthew Stafford leads a loaded offense featuring Davante Adams, Kyren Williams, and Blake Corum, alongside Puka Nacua (who should be active for at least half the season depending on his pending suspension). On defense, acquiring Myles Garrett from the Browns elevates the unit to another level, while rumors of Aaron Donald coming out of retirement make them even scarier.

The Rams end up playing a 10-7 Bears team in the Divisional Round. Fresh off their bye, the Rams assert dominance early against the Chicago Bears, winning by two-plus possessions.

In an electric rematch in the NFC Championship between the league's two best teams, the Rams' offense makes crucial late plays while Myles Garrett provides the key defensive stops to edge out Seattle and punch their ticket to Super Bowl LX.

## Super Bowl LX

As detailed in Part 1's AFC breakdown, the Jacksonville Jaguars navigate a gauntlet of an AFC postseason behind Trevor Lawrence's MVP year to represent the conference on the game's biggest stage. But while Jacksonville's run to the Super Bowl is a historic breakthrough, stopping the LA Rams on this stage is a different story.

The top-seeded Rams jump out to a hot start, building an early 17-7 lead. After stalling early, Jacksonville finally finds its footing late in the second quarter, driving downfield for a touchdown to cut the deficit to 17-14 heading into halftime. 

After halftime, the Rams take advantage of Jacksonville’s secondary not being able to keep up with Nacua and Adams and go up 24-14. Holding off the Jaguars’ offense for the rest of the third quarter, the Rams have a lead that feels very safe being up 27-17. 

Jacksonville makes one final push in the fourth quarter, orchestrating a touchdown drive to bring the game within three at 27-24. But Matthew Stafford and the Rams' offense respond immediately, marching downfield to find the end zone and pushing the lead back to two possessions at 34-24. Desperate to stay alive, Jacksonville quickly moves into field goal range to bring the score to 34-27, but their subsequent onside kick attempt falls incomplete. The Rams kneel out the final seconds to secure a 34-27 victory, claiming the third Super Bowl title in franchise history and their second since the 2021–2022 season.
