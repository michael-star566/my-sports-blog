## Most Valuable Player (MVP)
### Trevor Lawrence, QB, Jacksonville Jaguars

Lawrence finally makes the leap into the elite quarterback club, leading Jacksonville to a 14-3 record and taking the Jaguars to their first Super Bowl appearance in franchise history. Passing for 4,675 yards with 45 passing touchdowns on a 67% completion percentage, while adding 7 rushing touchdowns and 427 rushing yards. He also brings down his interception count from 12 last year to 8 this year.

## Offensive Player of the Year (OPOY)
### Jahmyr Gibbs, RB, Detroit Lions

Jahmyr Gibbs has been one of the most dominant backs in the league for the last few years, but because he split reps with David Montgomery, his stats didn't fully represent that. Now that Montgomery is gone, Gibbs will show why he is the best running back in the league and potentially the best skill position player in the league. Going for 1,424 rushing yards on 269 carries with an average of 5.29 yards per carry with 13 rushing touchdowns, he shows just how much he can dominate on the ground. Adding on to that with 63 receptions for 558 receiving yards and 6 receiving touchdowns, he ends the year with 1,982 scrimmage yards and 19 total touchdowns. 

## Defensive Player of the Year (DPOY)
### Myles Garrett, DE, Los Angeles Rams

Myles Garrett finally got out of Cleveland and has a chance to compete for a Super Bowl with the LA Rams. Being on a team that will be winning most games late, this gives Garrett even more opportunities to get to the opposing quarterback. This sets Garrett up for another historic season where he once again breaks the NFL sack record he set last season by posting 24.5 sacks. On top of that, he totals 67 tackles and 5 forced fumbles, leading to another DPOY award and helping the Rams make a push to the Super Bowl once again.

## Offensive Rookie of the Year (OROY)
### De’Zhaun Stribling, WR, San Francisco 49ers

Stribling has been dominant this preseason and doesn’t have many snaps with starter Brock Purdy. With Ricky Pearsall out for the year the 49ers will need someone to step up and fill that role. Stribling is a perfect fit for this offense and could be the perfect big play maker with veteran Mike Evans playing as more of a red zone threat. Going for 925 receiving yards on 70 receptions and 9 touchdowns, Stribling wins Offensive Rookie of the Year and helps the 49ers to a 12-5 record.

## Defensive Rookie of the Year (DROY)
### Caleb Downs, S, Dallas Cowboys

Downs was arguably the most NFL ready player going into this year’s draft. He fell to Dallas and will show everyone what the hype is all about. Dallas plans on using him all over the field and he will be a huge factor in run stopping and the secondary. Having 101 total tackles as a safety is incredible and pairing that with 8 interceptions, 2.5 sacks, and 2 forced fumbles, Downs instantly makes a huge impact for this Cowboys defense and easily takes Defensive Rookie of the Year.
