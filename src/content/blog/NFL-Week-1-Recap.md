<h2 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.75rem;">What Week 1 of the NFL Season Showed Us About the League</h2>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Intro</h3>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Kicking off 2026 with a 10–6 prediction record is a solid start, but if Week 1 proved anything, it’s that the NFL thrives on chaos.</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Between the 49ers steamrolling the Rams down in Melbourne, the Cardinals stunning the Chargers in LA, and Caleb Williams leading the Bears to a historic 59-point offensive explosion against Carolina, preseason expectations were instantly shattered. Even the games that felt safe, like Green Bay letting a massive lead evaporate in a 39–22 loss to Minnesota, proved that paper projections mean nothing once the ball is snapped.</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Now that the dust has settled, let’s look past the box scores, separate the early flukes from the genuine trends, and break down what this wild opening weekend told us about the rest of the season.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">New England Patriots at Seattle Seahawks</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Patriots 34–31 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Seahawks 13–10</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">What projected as a high-scoring duel devolved into an offensive slog. Seattle lost Sam Darnold to an early injury, forcing Drew Lock under center to manage the game. On the other side, New England lost A.J. Brown to an injury, while Romeo Doubs struggled to make an impact in his Patriots debut. Drake Maye had a rough outing with three critical interceptions, none more devastating than a red zone pick late in the fourth quarter when New England was well within field goal range to tie, ultimately sealing the Seahawks' win.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">San Francisco 49ers vs LA Rams (Melbourne)</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Rams 31–21 | <strong>Actual:</strong> 49ers 27–7</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">The Rams' decision to arrive in Australia just 28 hours before kickoff backfired as they came out utterly lifeless. Newly added Myles Garrett turned in a nothing burger of a game before it was revealed he needs knee surgery and is heading to Injured Reserve. Offensively, Los Angeles completely failed to find Davante Adams in the first half, rendering him a non-factor early. Meanwhile, San Francisco shook off an injury scare to exciting rookie De'Zhaun Stribling, who left the game twice, the second time for good with an ankle injury, and still cruised to an emphatic blowout win.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Cleveland Browns at Jacksonville Jaguars</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Jaguars 27–17 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Jaguars 34–10</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Jacksonville exceeded the lofty preseason hype. Trevor Lawrence orchestrated a passing masterclass, officially thrusting his name into early-season MVP conversations as he led an explosive, high-flying offense. On the flip side, Cleveland collapsed completely on both sides of the ball. The cries for change in Ohio are already deafening, with fans arguing the Browns desperately need to bench Deshaun Watson in favor of Shedeur Sanders. However, head coach Todd Monken shut down any immediate quarterback controversy by announcing that Watson will once again start next week.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Cincinnati Bengals</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Buccaneers 24–17 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Bengals 33–27</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Tampa Bay ultimately fell short because the offense completely shot itself in the foot with ball security. While the Bengals initially struggled to establish any offensive rhythm—with Ja'Marr Chase managing just 2 receptions for 12 yards—Tampa Bay's turnover woes proved fatal. Baker Mayfield had a nightmare afternoon handling the ball, coughing it up with 3 fumbles lost, while a Bucky Irving fumble brought Tampa Bay's total to 4 brutal turnovers on the day. Cincinnati's offense took full advantage to build a commanding lead, though Joe Burrow threw a late-game pick-six that made the final score appear much closer.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">New Orleans Saints at Detroit Lions</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Lions 31–24 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Lions 31–30</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">New Orleans quarterback Tyler Shough stumbled out of the gate with a turnover-prone first quarter, throwing 2 interceptions and coughing up a fumble lost as the Saints fell behind. However, Shough completely turned it around in the second half, mounting a furious comeback by throwing for 410 yards and 3 touchdowns, sparked by a massive 10-catch, 182-yard performance from Chris Olave and Devaughn Vele chipping in with 7 catches for 69 yards and a touchdown. Detroit leaned on Amon-Ra St. Brown, who hauled in 10 catches for 67 yards and 2 scores. Ultimately, Detroit's early cushion held up just enough, with Jahmyr Gibbs bruising the defense for 156 yards and 2 touchdowns to narrowly escape with the 31–30 win.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">New York Jets at Tennessee Titans</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Titans 21–17 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Jets 23–10</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">The Jets controlled this one on the road. Geno Smith directed the offense cleanly, while Breece Hall carried the attack out of the backfield with 22 carries for 102 yards and a touchdown, alongside 2 catches for 16 yards. Garrett Wilson led the receiving corps, hauling in 6 passes for 79 yards. Meanwhile, Cam Ward and the Titans' offense largely struggled to get anything going against a stout New York defense, failing to sustain rhythm all afternoon.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Baltimore Ravens at Indianapolis Colts</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Ravens 35–27 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Ravens 41–23</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Lamar Jackson and Derrick Henry completely shredded the defense, with Henry rumbling for 144 yards and 3 touchdowns while Jackson added 40 yards and a score on the ground alongside 324 passing yards. Zay Flowers had a massive game with 5 catches for 150 yards and a touchdown but unfortunately left early with an injury. For Indianapolis, Jonathan Taylor played to his usual dominant standard with 98 rushing yards and 2 scores, and Alec Pierce chipped in with 4 receptions for 61 yards, but the Colts fell behind early and couldn't generate enough momentum late to complete a comeback against Baltimore's firepower.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Atlanta Falcons at Pittsburgh Steelers</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Steelers 17–14 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Steelers 20–13</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">This played out as a gritty, low-scoring battle, though with a few unexpected twists. Tua Tagovailoa sat this one out with an injury, leaving Cooper Rush to fill in under center for Atlanta. Bijan Robinson served as Atlanta's lone functional offensive engine, grinding out 83 rushing yards while leading the team with 8 receptions for 90 yards and a touchdown. On the other side, Pittsburgh's offense stagnated and only found the end zone once through the air, but the defense and T.J. Watt won Pittsburgh the game, sealed by a massive T.J. Watt pick-six to secure the victory.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Chicago Bears at Carolina Panthers</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Bears 28–13 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Bears 59–37</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">This game turned into an absolute, high-flying scoring frenzy, especially in a wild first half where the teams combined for 48 points. Caleb Williams dominated under center, completing 21 of 29 passes for 269 yards and 2 touchdowns while adding 65 yards and 2 more scores on the ground. The Bears controlled the line of scrimmage with a massive rushing attack, racking up 291 total team yards and 6 touchdowns on the ground, paced by D'Andre Swift's 124 yards and 3 scores alongside Kyle Monangai's 100 yards and a touchdown. For Carolina, Jalen Coker had a monster breakout game, hauling in 8 catches for 138 yards and 2 touchdowns, while Bryce Young threw for 361 yards and 3 scores in a losing effort.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Buffalo Bills at Houston Texans</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Bills 21–17 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Bills 36–31</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">This turned into far more of an offensive fireworks show than projected, but Buffalo found ways to answer every punch. Josh Allen put on a dominant performance, throwing for 334 yards and 2 touchdowns while hurting Houston on the ground with 2 rushing scores. DJ Moore delivered an impactful Bills debut as well, hauling in 5 passes for 100 yards and a touchdown. Houston's offense moved the ball effectively overall, with the offseason addition of David Montgomery paying immediate dividends as he found the end zone 3 total times (2 rushing, 1 receiving). However, with a chance to drive down and take the lead late, the Texans simply couldn't execute on their final drive to steal the win.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Miami Dolphins at Las Vegas Raiders</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Raiders 24–21 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Raiders 27–13</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Las Vegas defended home turf decisively, while Miami’s offense struggled to find any rhythm as De'Von Achane got completely shut down on the ground. Despite the team's offensive woes, Caleb Douglas had a strong showing, beginning to emerge as Miami's reliable wide receiver one. On the other side, Kirk Cousins expertly orchestrated the attack with 3 passing touchdowns, though he also threw 2 picks on the afternoon. The true star of the day was Ashton Jeanty, who churned out 102 rushing yards while doing all of his touchdown damage through the air with 2 receiving scores; if he sustains this multi-phase production, he will force his way into the top-tier running back conversation.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Green Bay Packers at Minnesota Vikings</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Packers 27–20 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Vikings 39–22</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Minnesota started disastrously when Kyler Murray threw an early interception and then left with a concussion, forcing backup Carson Wentz into action. Green Bay capitalized, building a 19–10 halftime lead behind Christian Watson's explosive 6-catch, 147-yard, 2-touchdown performance. However, Justin Jefferson completely flipped the script, hauling in 8 catches for 92 yards and 2 scores to spark a stunning 22-point fourth-quarter avalanche. As Minnesota caught fire, Green Bay's offense collapsed. Without Josh Jacobs, whose absence due to legal issues severely hurt their rushing attack, the Packers became entirely one-dimensional, allowing the Vikings' defense to find its groove and slam the door shut.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Washington Commanders at Philadelphia Eagles</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Eagles 27–20 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Eagles 24–22</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">Philadelphia held on for a home win with an unexpected offensive script. DeVonta Smith had a surprisingly quiet Week 1, leaving emerging WR2 Dontayvion Wicks and tight end Dallas Goedert to carry the passing attack. On the ground, Saquon Barkley had a respectable 83-yard showing but fell short of his massive preseason expectations. Washington failed to generate early momentum before rallying behind strong performances from Stefon Diggs and Antonio Williams, offsetting Terry McLaurin being held to just 14 yards. A late touchdown pulled the Commanders within two, but a highly questionable two-point conversion play call left everyone watching at home wondering what the coaching staff was thinking, ultimately preserving Philly’s win.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Arizona Cardinals at LA Chargers</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Chargers 31–14 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Cardinals 26–14</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">The Cardinals’ defense completely flipped the expected script, exceeding expectations by stifling Los Angeles and keeping the offense from getting any sort of consistent rhythm going. Ladd McConkey was producing heavily for the Chargers, hauling in 5 catches for 82 yards and a touchdown, until he unfortunately went down with a rib injury in the third quarter. On the other side, Jacoby Brissett commanded the offense effortlessly under center for Arizona, completing 27 of 37 passes for 277 yards and a touchdown while picking apart the defense and finding his targets with ease on nearly every possession. In the backfield, Jerimiyah Love made the most of his opportunities, putting together a highly efficient outing with 41 yards and a rushing touchdown despite a limited 11-carry snap count.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Dallas Cowboys at New York Giants</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Giants 34–27 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Giants 28–20</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">New York's offense controlled this entire game. Dallas was able to score consistently with their limited opportunities, getting only two possessions in the second half, but early mistakes proved costly. After punting on their first drive of the game, Dak Prescott threw an ugly interception late in the first half, which led to the Giants going up 14–7 before halftime. For New York, Isaiah Likely had a great debut, operating as Jaxson Dart's ultimate safety net when his other guys weren't open while racking up 8 catches for 78 yards and 2 touchdowns. Cam Skattebo also shined in his return from the injury he sustained last year, churning out 81 yards and a touchdown on 18 carries.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.35rem;">Denver Broncos at Kansas City Chiefs</h3>

<p><strong>Prediction:</strong> Chiefs 24–14 | <strong>Actual:</strong> Chiefs 31–10</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2rem;">This game started as a grind with both offenses struggling to find their footing. Patrick Mahomes couldn't connect with his receivers consistently, but a massive 59-yard strike to Travis Kelce and a 19-yard Rashee Rice screen touchdown provided crucial passing sparks. Ultimately, newly acquired running back Kenneth Walker III was the clear difference-maker. Kansas City leaned heavily on him as he gashed a Denver defense struggling to regain last year's form, racking up 173 rushing yards on 23 carries, highlighted by a back-breaking 60-yard breakaway run. On the other side, Denver's offense stalled against Kansas City's front; Bo Nix coughed up two turnovers and mustered just a single 19-yard touchdown pass to Evan Engram, never giving the Broncos a chance to mount a real threat.</p>
